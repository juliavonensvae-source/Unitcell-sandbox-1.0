[words]
points-P: only at the 8 corners of the cell
points-I: at the 8 corners and one in the centre of the cell
points-F: at the 8 corners and in the centre of each of the 6 faces
points-C: at the 8 corners and in the centres of the two faces perpendicular to c
count-P: 8 corners × ⅛ = 1.
count-I: 8 corners × ⅛ + 1 centre point = 2.
count-F: 8 corners × ⅛ + 6 face centres × ½ = 4.
count-C: 8 corners × ⅛ + 2 face centres × ½ = 2.
same: {other} is equivalent to {plane}
different: {other} is <b>not</b> equivalent to {plane}
why-cubic: In cubic crystals every reordering of h, k and l, with any signs, gives an equivalent plane.
why-tetragonal: In tetragonal crystals h and k can swap places and every sign can change, but l stays in its place (c is the unique axis).
why-orthorhombic: In orthorhombic crystals only the signs can change: a, b and c all differ, so h, k and l never swap places.
why-hexagonal: In hexagonal crystals, written as (hkil) with i = −(h + k), h, k and i can be reordered and change sign together, and l can change sign on its own.
why-rhombohedral: On rhombohedral axes h, k and l can be reordered in any way, but their signs only change all together.
why-monoclinic: In monoclinic crystals (unique axis b) nothing can be reordered: k can change sign on its own, while h and l only change sign together.

[fixed]
level: easy
text: How many Bravais lattices are there in three dimensions?
answer: 14
explain: 14 lattices in 7 crystal systems: cubic (P, I, F), tetragonal (P, I), orthorhombic (P, C, I, F), hexagonal (P), rhombohedral (R), monoclinic (P, C) and triclinic (P).

[fixed]
level: easy
text: How many crystal systems are there in three dimensions?
answer: 7
explain: Cubic, tetragonal, orthorhombic, hexagonal, rhombohedral (trigonal), monoclinic and triclinic.

[fixed]
level: easy, medium
text: Which crystal system has the most Bravais lattices?
answer: Orthorhombic
wrong: Cubic | Tetragonal | Monoclinic
explain: Orthorhombic has four: P, C, I and F. Cubic has three (P, I, F), tetragonal and monoclinic two each.

[fixed]
level: medium, hard
text: Which of these is <b>not</b> one of the 14 Bravais lattices?
answer: Face-centred tetragonal
wrong: Body-centred tetragonal | Base-centred orthorhombic | Face-centred orthorhombic
explain: Face-centred tetragonal is the same lattice as body-centred tetragonal, drawn with a bigger cell, so it is not counted separately.

[fixed]
level: hard
text: Why is there no base-centred tetragonal Bravais lattice?
answer: It is the same lattice as simple tetragonal, described with a smaller cell
wrong: Centring the top and bottom faces would destroy the 4-fold axis | It is the same lattice as body-centred tetragonal | It is the same lattice as face-centred cubic
explain: The face-centre points make a smaller square net. A cell with edges a/√2 along the face diagonals is primitive and still tetragonal, so the lattice is simple tetragonal.

[fixed]
level: hard
text: Why is there no face-centred tetragonal Bravais lattice?
answer: It is the same lattice as body-centred tetragonal, described with a smaller cell
wrong: Centring all six faces would destroy the 4-fold axis | It is the same lattice as simple tetragonal | It is the same lattice as face-centred cubic
explain: Take a new cell with edges a/√2 along the face diagonals of the base. The face-centre points on the side faces then sit at the body centre of this smaller cell: it is body-centred tetragonal.

[fixed]
level: hard
text: Why is there no base-centred cubic Bravais lattice?
answer: Centring only one pair of faces destroys the cubic 3-fold axes, so the lattice is no longer cubic
wrong: It is the same lattice as simple cubic, described with a smaller cell | It is the same lattice as body-centred cubic | It is the same lattice as face-centred cubic
explain: With only the top and bottom faces centred, c is no longer equivalent to a and b, so the body diagonals stop being 3-fold axes. The lattice is simple tetragonal (with a smaller cell).
