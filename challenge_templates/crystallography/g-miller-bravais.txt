[fixed]
level: medium, hard
text: Why is the four-index (Miller–Bravais) notation used for hexagonal crystals?
answer: So that planes and directions that are equivalent by symmetry also have similar-looking indices
wrong: Because three indices cannot describe every plane in a hexagonal crystal | Because the c axis needs two indices | Because the hexagonal cell has four edges of equal length
explain: Three indices are enough, but equivalent planes can then look unrelated, e.g. $(100)$ and $(\bar110)$. With the third axis a3 they become $(10\bar10)$ and $(\bar1100)$: the same numbers in a different order.
