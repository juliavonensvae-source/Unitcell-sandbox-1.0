## Diagram: table of the 7 × 4 combinations (Bravais lattices topic): why a ✕ is not a new lattice
diagWhyCubicC: Only one pair of faces centred: the 3-fold axes along the body diagonals are lost, so the lattice is really tetragonal.
diagWhyTetragonalC: The same as tetragonal P: turn a and b by 45° to get a smaller primitive cell (a′ = a/√2).
diagWhyTetragonalF: The same as tetragonal I with a smaller cell (axes turned by 45°).
diagWhyHexagonalC: Breaks the 6-fold axis (the lattice becomes orthorhombic).
diagWhyHexagonalIF: Breaks the 6-fold axis.
diagWhyRhombohedralC: Breaks the 3-fold axis.
diagWhyRhombohedralIF: Gives another rhombohedral lattice with a smaller primitive cell (or a cubic one for special angles).
diagWhyMonoclinicI: The same as monoclinic C, with a different choice of a and c.
diagWhyMonoclinicF: The same as monoclinic C with a smaller cell.
diagWhyTriclinicC: There are no angles or lengths to protect, so a smaller primitive triclinic cell always exists.
diagWhyTriclinicIF: A smaller primitive triclinic cell always exists.
diagBravaisNote: Hover a ✕ to see why that combination is not a new lattice. For the rhombohedral system the “P” column is its primitive cell, R.

# 3D highlights (the caption under "Show in the 3D view")
## Spheres touching along a line (packing fraction)
hlEdge: edge
hlFaceDiagonal: face diagonal
hlBodyDiagonal: body diagonal
hlTouchCaption: The spheres touch along the <b>{what}</b> {dir}: {count} spheres in a row span {rel}.

## The other highlights
hlAxesCaption: The three lattice vectors from the origin, with α between <i>b</i> and <i>c</i>, β between <i>a</i> and <i>c</i>, γ between <i>a</i> and <i>b</i>.
hlVolumeCell: The parallelepiped spanned by <i>a</i>, <i>b</i> and <i>c</i>.
hlVolumePrism: The hexagonal prism: three primitive cells, volume 3<i>V</i>.
hlSharesCaption: Each point is labelled with the part of it that lies inside the highlighted cell.
hlCornersOnly: P: corners only
hlPrimitiveCaption: A primitive cell has lattice points on its corners only.
hlCentringCaption: The arrows are the centring vectors <b>t</b>: adding any of them to a lattice point gives another lattice point.
hlDirectionCaption: The arrow {dir} and the steps along <i>a</i>, <i>b</i>, <i>c</i> that build it.
hlPlaneCaption: The plane {plane} nearest the origin and the points where it meets the axes (an index 0 means it never meets that axis).
hlSpacingCaption: Two neighbouring {plane} planes and the distance <i>d</i> measured straight along their normal.
hlFamilyCaption: The {plane} planes that hold lattice points in the highlighted cell. Dashed ones lie between the planes of the formula (centred lattices).
hlNeighboursCaption: The point at the origin and its {count} nearest neighbours (some of them lie in the cells next door).
hlAngleDDCaption: The two directions from the origin and the angle between them.
hlAnglePPCaption: Planes are compared through their normals (the arrows), drawn here from the centre of the cell.
hlNormal: normal
hlZoneCaption: The two planes through the centre of the cell and the line where they cross.

## Interstitial sites and atom fit
hlSiteChoose: Choose {lattice} in the lattice picker to see this hole in the 3D view.
hlSiteCaption: The {kind} hole and its {cn} neighbours as touching spheres; the coloured sphere is the largest that fits.
hlSitesCoveredCounting: Interstitial sites are covered for cubic P, I and F. Choose one of them to see the counting in 3D.
hlSitesCoveredHoles: Interstitial sites are covered for cubic P, I and F. Choose one of them to see the holes in 3D.
hlSitesEvery: Every hole in the highlighted cell: {list}.
hlSiteSharesCaption: Each hole is labelled with the part of it that belongs to the highlighted cell: inside 1, on a face ½, on an edge ¼.
hlFitCubicOnly: The atom-fit tool works for cubic P, I and F. Choose one of them to see it in 3D.
hlFitFits: An atom with <i>r</i>/<i>R</i> = {ratio} fits: it does not reach any neighbour.
hlFitTooBig: An atom with <i>r</i>/<i>R</i> = {ratio} is too big: the red circles show where it cuts its neighbours, the arrows how far they must move.
