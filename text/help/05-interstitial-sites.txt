## Interstitial sites and radius ratios

### Popover
helpInterstitialsTitle: Interstitial sites and radius ratios
helpInterstitialsExplain: Between touching lattice spheres of radius R there are holes: octahedral sites (6 neighbours) and tetrahedral sites (4 neighbours), plus the cubic site in cubic P (8 neighbours). The largest sphere that fits without pushing the neighbours apart has radius r = d − R, where d is the distance from the centre of the hole to its nearest neighbours; r/R is the critical radius ratio.
helpInterstitialsFormula1Tag: Any site
helpInterstitialsFormula1: r_\text{max} = d - R\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad \frac{r}{R} = \frac{d}{R} - 1
helpInterstitialsFormula2Tag: Cubic F
helpInterstitialsFormula2: \text{oct: } \sqrt2 - 1 \approx 0.414,\qquad \text{tet: } \sqrt{\tfrac32} - 1 \approx 0.225
helpInterstitialsFormula3Tag: Cubic I
helpInterstitialsFormula3: \text{oct: } \tfrac{2}{\sqrt3} - 1 \approx 0.155,\qquad \text{tet: } \sqrt{\tfrac53} - 1 \approx 0.291
helpInterstitialsFormula4Tag: Cubic P
helpInterstitialsFormula4: \text{cubic site: } \sqrt3 - 1 \approx 0.732
helpInterstitialsSym1: R
helpInterstitialsSym1Text: radius of the lattice spheres (touching: d<sub>nn</sub>/2)
helpInterstitialsSym2: r
helpInterstitialsSym2Text: radius of the sphere in the hole
helpInterstitialsSym3: d
helpInterstitialsSym3Text: distance from the centre of the hole to the nearest lattice points
helpInterstitialsNow: {lattice}: not available yet. Interstitial sites are covered for cubic P, I and F.
helpInterstitialsNow2: {lattice}: {sites}. Add them with + Add → Interstitial sites.

[3D highlight: the "Show in the 3D view" box, above the tabs]

### SEE MORE: Derivation
[diagram: drawn at the top of the derivation]
helpInterstitialsStep1: The lattice spheres touch their nearest neighbours, so their radius is fixed by the lattice:
helpInterstitialsStep1Tex: R = \frac{d_\text{nn}}{2}
helpInterstitialsStep2: A sphere in a hole touches the spheres around it when the centre distance equals the sum of the radii. Any larger and it pushes them apart:
helpInterstitialsStep2Tex: d = R + r_\text{max}\ \Rightarrow\ \frac{r_\text{max}}{R} = \frac{d}{R} - 1
helpInterstitialsStep3: <b>Cubic F, octahedral</b> (body centre and edge midpoints, 4 per cell). Along a cell edge there are two spheres and the hole between them; the spheres touch along the face diagonal:
helpInterstitialsStep3Tex: a = 2R + 2r,\qquad \sqrt2\,a = 4R\ \Rightarrow\ 2r = 2\sqrt2R - 2R\ \Rightarrow\ \frac{r}{R} = \sqrt2 - 1 = 0.414
helpInterstitialsStep4: <b>Cubic F, tetrahedral</b> at (¼, ¼, ¼) (8 per cell): a quarter of the body diagonal from a corner sphere.
helpInterstitialsStep4Tex: R + r = \frac{\sqrt3}{4}a = \frac{\sqrt3}{4}\cdot2\sqrt2R = \sqrt{\tfrac32}\,R\ \Rightarrow\ \frac{r}{R} = \sqrt{\tfrac32} - 1 = 0.225
helpInterstitialsStep5: <b>Cubic I, octahedral</b> at the face centres and edge midpoints (6 per cell). The closest spheres are the two at a/2; the spheres touch along the body diagonal:
helpInterstitialsStep5Tex: R + r = \frac{a}{2},\qquad \sqrt3\,a = 4R\ \Rightarrow\ \frac{r}{R} = \frac{2}{\sqrt3} - 1 = 0.155
helpInterstitialsStep6: <b>Cubic I, tetrahedral</b> at (½, ¼, 0) (12 per cell): two corners and two body centres, all at the same distance:
helpInterstitialsStep6Tex: R + r = \sqrt{\big(\tfrac12\big)^2 + \big(\tfrac14\big)^2}\,a = \frac{\sqrt5}{4}a = \sqrt{\tfrac53}\,R\ \Rightarrow\ \frac{r}{R} = 0.291
helpInterstitialsStep7: <b>Cubic P, cubic site</b> at the body centre (1 per cell): the spheres touch along the edge, the hole is half the body diagonal away from each corner:
helpInterstitialsStep7Tex: a = 2R,\qquad R + r = \frac{\sqrt3}{2}a = \sqrt3\,R\ \Rightarrow\ \frac{r}{R} = \sqrt3 - 1 = 0.732
helpInterstitialsStep8: The cubic I octahedral hole is squashed: 2 neighbours at a/2 but 4 more at a/√2. That is why it is smaller than the tetrahedral hole in the same lattice. Each kind has its own “?” with the full derivation.

## Sites per cell and per lattice point

## Atom fit

### Popover
helpAtomFitExplain: Put an atom of radius r in a hole between lattice spheres of radius R. It fits without pushing anything when r ≤ r<sub>max</sub> = d − R, which is the same as r/R being at most the critical ratio of that site. A bigger atom overlaps its nearest neighbours, and each of them must move outward by δ = r + R − d to make room.
helpAtomFitFormula1: \text{fits} \iff r \le d - R \iff \frac{r}{R} \le \Big(\frac{r}{R}\Big)_\text{crit}
helpAtomFitFormula2: \delta_i = r + R - d_i\qquad(\text{only when } \delta_i > 0)
helpAtomFitSym1: r
helpAtomFitSym1Text: radius of the atom in the hole
helpAtomFitSym2: R
helpAtomFitSym2Text: radius of the lattice spheres (touching: d<sub>nn</sub>/2)
helpAtomFitSym3: d_i
helpAtomFitSym3Text: distance from the centre of the hole to neighbour i (d = the nearest)
helpAtomFitSym4: \delta_i
helpAtomFitSym4Text: how far neighbour i must move outward
helpAtomFitNow2: {lattice}: critical ratios {ratios}.
helpAtomFitNow3: {name}: r/R = {ratio} against {critical}, so it fits.
helpAtomFitNow3TooBig: {name}: r/R = {ratio} against {critical}, so it is too big.

helpAtomFitStep1: Two spheres just touch when the distance between their centres equals the sum of their radii; closer than that, they overlap.

helpAtomFitStep2: The lattice spheres touch each other, so their radius is half the nearest-neighbour distance:
helpAtomFitStep2Tex: R = \frac{d_\text{nn}}{2}
helpAtomFitStep3: The centre of the hole is a distance d from its nearest neighbours. The biggest atom that fits touches them:

helpAtomFitStep4: So an atom fits exactly when its ratio is not above the critical ratio. Because both r<sub>max</sub> and R scale with a, the ratio is the same for every lattice parameter.

# The five site topics (title, explanation, formulas, derivation, and the labels of the lines in the 3D highlight)
## Cubic P: the cubic site

helpSiteCPCubTitle: Cubic P: the cubic site
helpSiteCPCubExplain: Cubic P has one hole per cell, at the body centre, with the 8 corner spheres around it on the corners of a cube. It is a big hole: the largest sphere that fits has $r/R = \sqrt3 - 1 \approx 0.732$. The faces of the cube are only windows from one hole to the next.
helpSiteCPCubFormula1Tex: a = 2R,\qquad \sqrt3\,a = 2R + 2r
helpSiteCPCubFormula2Tex: \frac{r}{R} = \sqrt3 - 1 \approx 0.732
helpSiteCPCubRtex: \tfrac12 a
helpSiteCPCubDtex: \tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}a
helpSiteCPCubStep1: In cubic P the spheres touch along the <b>cell edge</b>: two corner spheres side by side span one edge.
helpSiteCPCubStep1Tex: a = 2R
helpSiteCPCubStep2: The body centre is half a body diagonal from every corner, so all 8 neighbours are the same distance away:
helpSiteCPCubStep2Tex: d = \tfrac12\sqrt3\,a
helpSiteCPCubStep3: A sphere in the hole that just fits touches all 8. Along the <b>body diagonal</b> lie a corner sphere, the hole and the opposite corner sphere:
helpSiteCPCubStep3Tex: \sqrt3\,a = R + 2r + R = 2R + 2r
helpSiteCPCubStep4: Put in a = 2R and solve for r:
helpSiteCPCubStep4Tex: 2\sqrt3\,R = 2R + 2r\ \Rightarrow\ r = (\sqrt3 - 1)\,R
helpSiteCPCubStep5: Divide by R. The ratio does not depend on a, so it is the same for every cubic P lattice:
helpSiteCPCubStep5Tex: \frac{r}{R} = \sqrt3 - 1 \approx 0.732
helpSiteCPCubStep6: Counting: the body centre belongs to this cell alone.
helpSiteCPCubStep6Tex: N_\text{cub} = 1\ \text{per cell} = 1\ \text{per lattice point}
helpSiteCPCubHlLabel1: 2<i>R</i> + 2<i>r</i> = √3 <i>a</i>
helpSiteCPCubHlLabel2: 2<i>R</i> = <i>a</i>

## Cubic I: octahedral site

helpSiteCIOctTitle: Cubic I: octahedral site
helpSiteCIOctExplain: In cubic I the octahedral holes sit at the face centres and edge midpoints, 6 per cell (3 per lattice point). Their octahedron is squashed: 2 neighbours are only a/2 away while the other 4 are a/√2 away. The 2 close ones limit the hole to $r/R = 2/\sqrt3 - 1 \approx 0.155$.
helpSiteCIOctFormula1Tex: R + r = \frac{a}{2},\qquad \sqrt3\,a = 4R
helpSiteCIOctFormula2Tex: \frac{r}{R} = \frac{2}{\sqrt3} - 1 \approx 0.155
helpSiteCIOctRtex: \tfrac{\sqrt3}{4}a
helpSiteCIOctDtex: \tfrac12 a
helpSiteCIOctStep1: In cubic I the spheres touch along the <b>body diagonal</b>: corner, body centre, opposite corner.
helpSiteCIOctStep1Tex: \sqrt3\,a = 4R\ \Rightarrow\ R = \tfrac{\sqrt3}{4}a
helpSiteCIOctStep2: Take the hole at the edge midpoint $(\tfrac12, 0, 0)$. The two corner spheres on that edge are a/2 away; the four body-centre spheres around the edge are further:
helpSiteCIOctStep2Tex: d_\text{corner} = \tfrac12 a,\qquad d_\text{body centre} = \sqrt{\big(\tfrac12\big)^2 + \big(\tfrac12\big)^2}\,a = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}a
helpSiteCIOctStep3: The closest neighbours decide how big the hole is. Along the <b>cell edge</b>:
helpSiteCIOctStep3Tex: a = R + 2r + R\ \Rightarrow\ R + r = \tfrac12 a
helpSiteCIOctStep4: Divide by R:
helpSiteCIOctStep4Tex: \frac{r}{R} = \frac{a/2}{\sqrt3\,a/4} - 1 = \frac{2}{\sqrt3} - 1 \approx 0.155
helpSiteCIOctStep5: The 4 far neighbours alone would allow a sphere 4 times bigger, so the hole is a <b>squashed octahedron</b>. An atom that is too big pushes only the 2 close neighbours apart and stretches the lattice along one axis.
helpSiteCIOctStep5Tex: \frac{a/\sqrt2}{R} - 1 = \frac{2\sqrt2}{\sqrt3} - 1 \approx 0.633
helpSiteCIOctStep6: Counting: 6 face centres shared by 2 cells and 12 edge midpoints shared by 4.
helpSiteCIOctStep6Tex: N_\text{oct} = 6\cdot\tfrac12 + 12\cdot\tfrac14 = 6 = 3\ \text{per lattice point}
helpSiteCIOctHlLabel1: <i>a</i> = 2<i>R</i> + 2<i>r</i>
helpSiteCIOctHlLabel2: 2<i>R</i> = √3 <i>a</i>/2

## Cubic I: tetrahedral site

helpSiteCITetTitle: Cubic I: tetrahedral site
helpSiteCITetExplain: In cubic I the tetrahedral holes sit at (½, ¼, 0)-type positions, 4 on each face, 12 per cell (6 per lattice point). Each has 4 neighbours at the same distance: two corners and two body centres. The largest sphere that fits has $r/R = \sqrt{5/3} - 1 \approx 0.291$, bigger than the octahedral hole of the same lattice.
helpSiteCITetFormula1Tex: R + r = \frac{\sqrt5}{4}a,\qquad \sqrt3\,a = 4R
helpSiteCITetFormula2Tex: \frac{r}{R} = \sqrt{\tfrac53} - 1 \approx 0.291
helpSiteCITetRtex: \tfrac{\sqrt3}{4}a
helpSiteCITetDtex: \tfrac{\sqrt5}{4}a
helpSiteCITetStep1: The spheres touch along the <b>body diagonal</b>:
helpSiteCITetStep1Tex: \sqrt3\,a = 4R\ \Rightarrow\ R = \tfrac{\sqrt3}{4}a
helpSiteCITetStep2: The hole at $(\tfrac12, \tfrac14, 0)$ has the corners $(0,0,0)$ and $(1,0,0)$ and the body centres $(\tfrac12,\tfrac12,\pm\tfrac12)$ around it. All four are equally far:
helpSiteCITetStep2Tex: d = \sqrt{\big(\tfrac12\big)^2 + \big(\tfrac14\big)^2}\,a = \sqrt{\big(\tfrac14\big)^2 + \big(\tfrac12\big)^2}\,a = \tfrac{\sqrt5}{4}a
helpSiteCITetStep3: A sphere that just fits touches all four:
helpSiteCITetStep3Tex: R + r = \tfrac{\sqrt5}{4}a
helpSiteCITetStep4: Divide by R:
helpSiteCITetStep4Tex: \frac{r}{R} = \frac{\sqrt5/4}{\sqrt3/4} - 1 = \sqrt{\tfrac53} - 1 \approx 0.291
helpSiteCITetStep5: So in cubic I the tetrahedral hole is the larger one (0.291 against 0.155), the opposite of cubic F. Small atoms in a body-centred lattice often prefer it.
helpSiteCITetStep6: Counting: 4 holes on each of the 6 faces, each shared by 2 cells.
helpSiteCITetStep6Tex: N_\text{tet} = 24\cdot\tfrac12 = 12 = 6\ \text{per lattice point}
helpSiteCITetHlLabel1: <i>R</i> + <i>r</i> = √5 <i>a</i>/4
helpSiteCITetHlLabel2: 2<i>R</i> = √3 <i>a</i>/2

## Cubic F: octahedral site

helpSiteCFOctTitle: Cubic F: octahedral site
helpSiteCFOctExplain: In cubic F the octahedral holes sit at the body centre and the 12 edge midpoints, 4 per cell (1 per lattice point). Each is surrounded by 6 neighbours at a/2, on the corners of a regular octahedron. The largest sphere that fits touches all six, which gives $r/R = \sqrt2 - 1 \approx 0.414$.
helpSiteCFOctFormula1Tex: a = 2R + 2r,\qquad \sqrt2\,a = 4R
helpSiteCFOctFormula2Tex: \frac{r}{R} = \sqrt2 - 1 \approx 0.414
helpSiteCFOctRtex: \tfrac{\sqrt2}{4}a
helpSiteCFOctDtex: \tfrac12 a
helpSiteCFOctStep1: In cubic F the spheres touch along the <b>face diagonal</b>: a corner sphere, the face-centre sphere and the opposite corner sphere sit in a row.
helpSiteCFOctStep1Tex: \sqrt2\,a = 4R\ \Rightarrow\ a = 2\sqrt2\,R
helpSiteCFOctStep2: Along a <b>cell edge</b> the two corner spheres have the octahedral hole between them, at the edge midpoint. A sphere that just fits touches both:
helpSiteCFOctStep2Tex: a = R + 2r + R = 2R + 2r
helpSiteCFOctStep3: Both are the same length a, so set them equal:
helpSiteCFOctStep3Tex: 2R + 2r = 2\sqrt2\,R\ \Rightarrow\ r = (\sqrt2 - 1)\,R
helpSiteCFOctStep4: Divide by R. The ratio does not depend on a: every cubic F lattice has the same one.
helpSiteCFOctStep4Tex: \frac{r}{R} = \sqrt2 - 1 \approx 0.414
helpSiteCFOctStep5: Check with the body-centre hole: its 6 neighbours are the face centres, all a/2 away, so the octahedron is regular and gives the same answer.
helpSiteCFOctStep5Tex: R + r = \tfrac12 a = \sqrt2\,R
helpSiteCFOctStep6: Counting: the body centre belongs to the cell alone, each of the 12 edge midpoints is shared by 4 cells.
helpSiteCFOctStep6Tex: N_\text{oct} = 1 + 12\cdot\tfrac14 = 4 = 1\ \text{per lattice point}
helpSiteCFOctHlLabel1: <i>a</i> = 2<i>R</i> + 2<i>r</i>
helpSiteCFOctHlLabel2: √2 <i>a</i> = 4<i>R</i>

## Cubic F: tetrahedral site

helpSiteCFTetTitle: Cubic F: tetrahedral site
helpSiteCFTetExplain: In cubic F the tetrahedral holes sit at the 8 positions of the (¼, ¼, ¼) type, all inside the cell: 2 per lattice point. Each one has 4 neighbours (a corner and the three face centres next to it) on the corners of a regular tetrahedron. The largest sphere that fits has $r/R = \sqrt{3/2} - 1 \approx 0.225$.
helpSiteCFTetFormula1Tex: R + r = \frac{\sqrt3}{4}a,\qquad \sqrt2\,a = 4R
helpSiteCFTetFormula2Tex: \frac{r}{R} = \sqrt{\tfrac32} - 1 \approx 0.225
helpSiteCFTetRtex: \tfrac{\sqrt2}{4}a
helpSiteCFTetDtex: \tfrac{\sqrt3}{4}a
helpSiteCFTetStep1: The spheres touch along the <b>face diagonal</b>:
helpSiteCFTetStep1Tex: \sqrt2\,a = 4R\ \Rightarrow\ a = 2\sqrt2\,R
helpSiteCFTetStep2: The hole at $(\tfrac14, \tfrac14, \tfrac14)$ lies a quarter of the way along the <b>body diagonal</b>. Its neighbours $(0,0,0)$, $(\tfrac12,\tfrac12,0)$, $(\tfrac12,0,\tfrac12)$ and $(0,\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$ are all equally far:
helpSiteCFTetStep2Tex: d = \sqrt{3\cdot\big(\tfrac14\big)^2}\,a = \tfrac{\sqrt3}{4}a
helpSiteCFTetStep3: A sphere that just fits touches all four:
helpSiteCFTetStep3Tex: R + r = \tfrac{\sqrt3}{4}a = \tfrac{\sqrt3}{4}\cdot 2\sqrt2\,R = \sqrt{\tfrac32}\,R
helpSiteCFTetStep4: Divide by R:
helpSiteCFTetStep4Tex: \frac{r}{R} = \sqrt{\tfrac32} - 1 \approx 0.225
helpSiteCFTetStep5: Four neighbours crowd in closer than six can, so in cubic F the tetrahedral hole is the smaller one (0.225 against 0.414).
