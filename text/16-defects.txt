# Dislocations

## Names, + Add entries and command help (plain text: no HTML)
dislEdgeTitle: Edge dislocation
dislEdgeMenu: Edge dislocation
dislEdgeDesc: An extra half-plane of atoms that ends at a line; b is at right angles to the line.
dislScrewTitle: Screw dislocation
dislScrewMenu: Screw dislocation
dislScrewDesc: The lattice planes join into a spiral ramp around a line; b runs along the line.

dislAfterEdge: It has left the crystal: the half above the slip plane has slipped by b = {b} Å over the half below, and a step of that height shows on each side.
dislAfterScrew: It has left the crystal: the two halves have slipped by b = {b} Å along the line, and a step shows on the end faces.
dislBurgers: Burgers vector
dislBLength: |<i>b</i>|
dislLine: Line along
dislAngle: <i>b</i> and the line

dislParallel: parallel: screw
dislLineAlong: The line runs along
dislHalfSide: Extra half-plane

dislBelow: below
dislHand: Screw

dislPosition: Position along {axis} (cells)
dislPlane: Slip plane at {axis} = (cells)
dislGlide: Glide through the crystal
dislMiddle: Back to the middle
dislGlideHintEdge: A shear stress τ pushes the line along b: it enters on one side, crosses the block and leaves a slip step of b.
dislGlideHintScrew: A shear stress τ pushes the line sideways, at right angles to b: once it has crossed, the two halves have slipped by b along the line.
dislMag: Displacement magnitude
dislMagHint: 100 % is a whole Burgers vector. Less shows the field growing; the atoms across the slip plane line up again only at 100 %, which is why b must be a lattice vector.
dislNu: Poisson's ratio ν (× 100)

dislShowCore: Show the core

bcDesc: Walk atom to atom around a dislocation and compare with the same walk in a perfect crystal: the gap is b.

## Card

bcIsEdge: b is at right angles to the line: an edge dislocation.

bcBack: Back
bcNext: Next
bcPlay: Play

bcAll: Show all
bcSize: Steps per side
bcMove: Move the circuit
bcCentre: Around the line
bcMoveHint: Move it off the dislocation and it closes: only a circuit around a dislocation fails.
bcCompare: Show the same circuit in a perfect crystal
bcCompareHint: Drawn beside the block, step for step.
bcRule: Burgers vector
bcRuleHint: A right-handed circuit about the line (anticlockwise when the line points at you), the same number of steps each way. In a perfect crystal it closes; around the dislocation the gap from the start S to the finish F is b.

vcLabel: Vacancy concentration
vcDesc: How many vacancies there are at a temperature: Nv = N exp(−Qv / kT), with a live graph.

## Card
vcSummary: <i>N</i><sub>v</sub>/<i>N</i> = {frac} at {T} K
vcT: Temperature T ({c} °C)
vcQ: Energy to form a vacancy Qᵥ ({kj} kJ/mol)
vcOneIn: One vacancy for every … atoms
vcInBlock: Expected in the block ({n} atoms)
vcPlot: Graph
vcPlotT: Nᵥ/N against T
vcPlotArrhenius: ln(Nᵥ/N) against 1/T
vcPlotTHint: A log scale: each grid line is a factor of 100 000. The dot is the temperature you chose; hover over the graph to read other values.
vcPlotArrheniusHint: An Arrhenius plot: a straight line of slope −Qᵥ/k, which is how Qᵥ is measured.
vcFormula: The equilibrium number of vacancies
vcFormulaHint: N = atoms per m³ of the lattice on screen (atoms per cell ÷ a³); k = 8.617 × 10⁻⁵ eV/K.

vcAxisFraction: Nᵥ / N

vcGraphT: The fraction of empty lattice points against temperature
vcGraphArrhenius: The logarithm of the fraction of empty lattice points against 1/T

## Input bar messages

# Formulas on the cards and in the help (LaTeX)
formulaRelax: u(r) = u_1\left(\frac{r_1}{r}\right)^2,\qquad u_1 = s\,\delta
formulaRelaxDelta: \delta = r + R - d
formulaRelaxVacancy: \delta_\text{vacancy} = -\frac{R}{10}
formulaEdgeUx: u_x = \frac{b}{2\pi}\left[\theta + \frac{xy}{2(1-\nu)(x^2+y^2)}\right]
formulaEdgeUy: u_y = -\frac{b}{2\pi}\left[\frac{1-2\nu}{4(1-\nu)}\ln\frac{x^2+y^2}{b^2} + \frac{x^2-y^2}{4(1-\nu)(x^2+y^2)}\right]
formulaScrew: u_z = \frac{b\,\theta}{2\pi}
formulaBurgers: \mathbf b = \overrightarrow{\text{SF}}\quad(\text{the same circuit closes in a perfect crystal})
formulaVacancyConc: N_\text{v} = N\exp\!\left(-\frac{Q_\text{v}}{kT}\right)
